Care & health/Koi behavior

Which koi came back to the familiar hand?

Maggie made contact in every recorded session. Gabriel logged the most separate contacts. Follow seven individual koi through the same experiment and see how those two findings fit together.

One hand, seven fish

At a community aquaponics site in New York City, a researcher rested her hand in the water. Seven adult koi could swim up or move away. They had become familiar with the researcher over several months. Food was given between sessions, away from the observation itself.

The researchers filmed roughly 20 minutes on arrival, before feeding, and again 20–30 minutes after the fish had eaten. A brush, bump or mouth contact initiated by a fish counted as touch. Swimming within reach was recorded separately. Two after-feeding recordings were lost, leaving 10 before and eight after.

Two ways to read the same behavior

Maggie returned in all 18 recorded sessions. Gabriel returned in 17, yet made 213 separate contacts. On 11 October alone he made 45 before feeding and 18 after. Asking which fish returned most reliably gives a different answer from asking which made the most contacts.

All seven fish lived in one tank and met the same person. They had experienced hand-feeding during familiarization. An approach could reflect an expectation of food, curiosity or another motivation. This study could not test whether a fish recognized its owner among strangers.

Try watching from the pond edge

Choose a koi you can identify by its markings. At ordinary feeding times, note whether it comes to the edge before and after a meal. Return to the same spot on a few different days. A note like “came to the edge twice” will be easier to compare than calling a fish friendly or shy. Leave it room to swim away.

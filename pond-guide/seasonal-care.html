Seasonal pond care

Koi care for winter, mild climates and summer

Start with your pond's conditions, not the month on the calendar. Northern and Southern Hemisphere seasons differ, and a subtropical pond needs a different plan from one exposed to prolonged freezing. Choose the route that matches your measured water temperature and local weather.

Cold-winter preparation

Use this route where extended frost or ice is a realistic risk, in either hemisphere. A shallow or exposed above-ground pond needs particular assessment; a single depth figure cannot guarantee safe outdoor overwintering.

  • Clear waste before the freeze. Remove fallen leaves and accumulated solids, clear skimmers and service mechanical filters. Preserve the established biological filter rather than stripping the whole system clean.
  • Plan frost protection around the installed equipment. Check the manuals for pumps, filters and exposed pipework. If a component must be shut down or drained, arrange the remaining filtration and oxygen supply first. Do not stop all life-support equipment simply because it is winter.
  • Keep a route for gas exchange. Where ice forms, use a suitable pond de-icer or a winter aeration arrangement specified for your setup. A de-icer opening does not prove the whole pond is warm enough. Avoid arrangements that unnecessarily mix away a stable deeper-water refuge. Do not hammer the ice or pour boiling water into the pond.
  • Prepare for an outage. Check backup air, alarms and access to the pond. If the pond may freeze through, plan protected or indoor accommodation with suitable filtration and aeration before the cold arrives, with local professional advice.

These winter checks draw on RSPCA fish-care advice, RHS pond care and OASE equipment guidance. Device positioning and shutdown instructions depend on the actual system.

Mild and subtropical winters

Little or no ice does not mean nothing changes. Cold fronts can still lower water temperature, especially in small, shallow or exposed ponds.

  • Keep filtration, water testing and fish observation going. Review exposed plumbing if a frost is forecast, even where most winters are mild.
  • Adjust feeding to the measured water-temperature trend, appetite and the food's instructions. A brief warm afternoon is not a reason to return immediately to summer feeding.
  • Continue removing leaves and checking water level. Check equipment and water quality again after cold fronts or heavy rain.

Warm season and summer in the Southern Hemisphere

When your area is warming up, use a warm-season plan rather than a Northern Hemisphere winter calendar. This also applies to Northern Hemisphere summers; altitude and local weather can change which route fits.

  • Check oxygen and aeration. Warm water holds less dissolved oxygen. In planted or algae-rich ponds, oxygen may fall overnight and be lowest around dawn. Keep the oxygen supply reliable overnight and during outages; daytime bubbles alone do not confirm adequate oxygen.
  • Manage heat and feeding together. Provide suitable shade without sealing the surface. Do not automatically increase food during a heatwave; consider fish behaviour, the feed label, oxygen and filter capacity.
  • Watch water level and storms. Check pump intakes and returns as evaporation lowers the level. Use appropriately treated replacement water and avoid abrupt temperature changes. Recheck equipment after storms or power loss.

The oxygen principles are supported by USGS and Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission. They describe general aquatic systems, not a universal koi temperature or oxygen threshold.

Feeding as water temperature changes

Measure the pond water rather than using air temperature or a date to set feeding. Reduce the amount as fish slow down, remove uneaten food, and follow the current instructions for the specific food and your overwintering plan. Published low-temperature advice differs: JNPA's general care guidance and the Hikari Wheat-Germ product instructions are different kinds of guidance. A specialist feed label is not a universal rule for every koi, pond or food. An unheated pond and a deliberately heated system need different plans.

Your seasonal check record

Keep a short log of water temperature at consistent times and locations, food offered and eaten, water-test results, water level, ice or heat conditions, and pump/air operation. Record changes before and after cold fronts, heatwaves and outages. Continue with the filtration guide and leaf-control checklist.

Reduced activity can accompany cold water, but gasping, rapid breathing, loss of balance or a sudden change should not be dismissed as “winter sleep.” Check oxygen supply, circulation and water quality promptly, and seek an aquatic veterinarian if fish appear unwell.

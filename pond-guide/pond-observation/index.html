After the storm: a koi pond observation log

Is the water level falling, or was it already low yesterday? A mark on the wall and a dated note can answer a question a live sensor reading cannot.

My pond observation log

Two surprises in a restored pond

The keeper behind Project Shangri-La inherited a cracked concrete pond. Several attempts to seal it failed. After fitting a liner, the keeper changed how water entered the pond and used a sensor to track its level. When heavy rain pushed water underneath the liner, parts of it lifted. This time the water was coming in under the lining. Before, it had been escaping through the concrete.

Later, the remote monitor sent live readings as the owner left on a trip, then stopped updating. The dashboard could no longer say what was happening at the pond. In your own notes, put the time of the last reading beside the time you actually looked. A reading from yesterday can look surprisingly convincing today.

What to write at the water’s edge

  • Water line · Choose a fixed mark, such as the lower edge of one stone. Record the distance above or below it and include the unit.
  • Flow and weather · Watch the return where it meets the surface. Note whether it looks like the usual flow and whether there was rain, a top-up or maintenance since your last visit.
  • The fish · Stand in the same spot for a minute. Write down what you saw and how many fish did it. For example, “Three koi stayed under the bridge while two crossed the open water.”

Example entry

Saturday, 08:10 · Water 2 cm below the mark on the flat stone. Return flowing. Heavy rain overnight. I will check the same mark this evening.

Pond Guide

Aquatic plants for koi ponds

You can keep a koi pond without aquatic plants. If you want greenery and shade, start with a protected basket at the edge or a separate planted area. Leave the main swimming area open so you can see the fish and remove waste.

A dedicated pond without plants

An open swimming area makes it easier to see the fish, inspect the bottom and remove waste. If the pond needs shade, consider a shade sail or a planted area outside the main pond. Keep access to the pond and equipment clear.

Planting baskets at the pond edge

Marginal plants grow near the edge. Emergent plants have stems and leaves above water. Set a stable, removable basket on a shelf that suits the plant’s recommended depth. Contain the planting medium and keep baskets clear of intakes and places you need to reach for cleaning.

Water lilies, free-floating plants and submerged plants

Water lilies root in the substrate and spread leaves across the surface. Free-floating plants do not anchor to the bottom. Submerged plants grow below the surface. Choose water lilies for the actual depth and available space. Keep a clear view of the fish and open water around the skimmer and returns. Plants do not replace aeration.

A separate planted filter

If you use a planted gravel bed as an additional stage, remove solids upstream and provide a way to access and clean the bed. Gravel can clog with trapped debris. Keep mechanical filtration and size the main system for the fish load.

Before adding plants

Check the plant’s scientific name, mature size, planting depth and suitability for your climate. Check local rules on invasive species. Rinse and inspect new plants separately for attached animals and plant fragments. Keep nursery water, discarded plants and trimmings out of waterways and storm drains.

A simple starting point

Try one basket where koi cannot reach the roots. Watch for loose planting medium, trapped leaves and growth toward the skimmer. Adjust or move the basket while it is still easy to lift.

Seasonal maintenance

  • Spring. Check baskets and split crowded plants when appropriate for the species.
  • Summer. Thin overgrowth and keep the skimmer, returns and aeration working.
  • Autumn. Remove fallen leaves and check the pond after storms.
  • Winter. Remove dying foliage and follow the plant’s cold-weather needs.

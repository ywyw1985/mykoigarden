How to measure your pond

Measure from the inside edge of the water to the opposite edge. For an oval, take the full end-to-end length and the full width through the centre, perpendicular to the length. For a circle, enter the same diameter twice.

Include the shallow shelves as well as the deeper centre. Take depth readings across the pond, giving larger areas more readings. In four equal-area sections with depths of 0.6, 0.8, 1.0 and 1.2 m, the average is 0.9 m.

A kidney shape, wide shelf or stream can make a single outline misleading. Divide the pool into simpler, non-overlapping sections and add their estimates. Water in external filters and pipes is separate.

Worked example

For a rectangle, volume in cubic metres equals length × width × average depth. For an ellipse, multiply those same three full measurements by π/4. With measurements in metres, multiply cubic metres by 1,000 to get litres.

A rectangular pond measuring 4 m × 2.5 m with a 0.9 m average depth holds about 9 m³, or 9,000 L (2,378 US gal). A 10% change represents 900 L (238 US gal). An oval with the same dimensions holds about 7,069 L, of which 10% is about 707 L.

The percentage is yours to choose for a volume calculation. The example does not prescribe a water-change schedule. Small differences in depth can materially change the estimate.

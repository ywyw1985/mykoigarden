Construction

Pond materials and build methods

Material choice affects cost, durability, shape, depth, repairability, and how easily filtration can be integrated.

EPDM liner pond

Flexible and common for home builds. It allows custom shapes and is easier than concrete for many DIY projects. Use underlayment, avoid sharp rocks, and protect folds from trapping waste. For koi, 45 mil EPDM is a common premium liner thickness because it balances flexibility with puncture resistance.

Concrete pond

Durable and clean-looking when built correctly. It supports vertical walls, bottom drains, and formal shapes. It requires proper sealing, curing, plumbing planning, and skilled construction. Fresh concrete is alkaline; the pond must be properly cured and sealed before koi are introduced, and pH should be monitored during startup.

Fiberglass or coated pond

Often used over concrete or block structures to create a smooth waterproof shell. It can be excellent for dedicated koi ponds, but installation quality matters. A good coating should be fish-safe after curing, continuous around pipe penetrations, and smooth enough that waste cannot collect in rough pores.

Preformed pond shell

Easy for small decorative ponds. Most are too shallow or too small for long-term koi keeping, but they can work for small water gardens or temporary holding with proper filtration. Check actual gallons, not product marketing language; many shells sold for "pond fish" are unsuitable for adult koi.

Block wall or raised construction

Good for formal raised ponds. Structural strength, waterproofing, insulation, and safe coping edges need attention. Water weighs about 8.34 lb per gallon, so even a modest raised pond creates major outward pressure on walls.

Rock-bottom ponds

Attractive, but rocks trap waste and make cleaning harder. For koi health, smooth cleanable surfaces usually win over decorative rock bottoms. If rocks are used, keep them out of deep fish zones and design drains or cleanout access so debris does not become a permanent sludge bed.

Plumbing details to decide early

  • Bottom drain size and location before liner or concrete work begins.
  • Skimmer placement based on prevailing wind and surface debris.
  • Separate valves for drains, skimmers, UV bypass, and filter cleaning.
  • Safe electrical service with GFCI/RCD protection and weather-rated equipment.
  • Overflow route so rain does not flood filters or wash untreated runoff into the pond.

Pond Planning

Koi Pond Types and Planning

Choose a pond around the fish you expect to keep, your climate and how you will clean and service it. A raised pond can also be a dedicated koi pond, while quarantine serves a separate purpose.

Chinese garden pond photographs

Look at the edges, planting and viewing angles in these photographs. Filtration capacity and construction details require separate verification.

Dedicated koi pond

A dedicated pond puts fish observation and maintenance first. Plan access to the pond floor, drains and filtration before deciding how the edges will look.

Garden pond with koi

Plant shelves, stones and waterfalls can be part of the design. Leave space to observe the fish and reach the spots where leaves and sediment collect.

Raised koi pond

Raising the rim can improve viewing and reduce excavation. Have the base and walls assessed for the water load and plan safe access, overflow and winter protection for your site.

Naturalistic pond

A planted layout can provide shade and a softer edge. Allow for plant maintenance, circulation and predator protection. Judge the filtration and aeration needs from the fish load and water conditions.

Separate quarantine tank

Give new fish a separate observation system with its own water, filtration, aeration and equipment. Keep nets and buckets separate from those used for resident fish. Seek advice from an aquatic veterinarian when illness is suspected.

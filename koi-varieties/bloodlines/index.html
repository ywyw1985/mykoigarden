How to read a koi bloodline

One family, two varieties

Sumire, a red-and-white Kohaku, and Tulip, a three-colour Taisho Sanke, share a grandmother. Sakai Fish Farm’s 2016 records name her Beauty Rose. Follow one line through Beauty IV to Sumire and the other through Big Rose to Tulip.

Four things a koi record can tell you

Variety
The appearance category. Sumire is a Kohaku.
Breeder
The person or farm that produced the fish. Record the owner separately when a show also names one.
Bloodline · keitō
A breeding line in which selected traits recur across generations. Beauty Rose is the line named on Sumire’s entry.
Parent koi · oyagoi
An individual fish used for breeding. Sumire is both a daughter in the chart and a mother in a later spawning record.

Japan’s Agency for Cultural Affairs, printed p. 83 (PDF)

Whose parent was Napoleon?

On printed page 20, Beauty IV appears in the entry for Sumire’s own parentage. Next to Sumire’s spawning date, 20 June 2016, the farm describes the male used that day as a son of Napoleon. Sumire was the mother of that batch. Napoleon appears one generation further back on the sire’s side. The catalogue gives no personal name for the male.

How the Sensuke Kohaku line travelled

The chairman of the Matsue Nishikigoi Center traces Sensuke (仙助), a Kohaku line, to a cross in Niigata around 1960. He recalls the strong frames and crisp edges of the red markings on early fish. He brought descendants to Matsue and later paired them with a Dainichi male. Several offspring helped establish the Matsue line. One, Sakura, went to Sakai as a brood fish.

What does a photo label establish?

KoiQuestion credits Dainichi as breeder of two young koi. The caption for the 28 cm Sanke specifies the Hotaru bloodline. The caption for the roughly 33 cm Showa leaves the bloodline field empty. For the Showa, the caption supports a breeder attribution only.

What a judge can see

A family history can tell you why a breeder paid attention to a particular frame or colour. At a show, judges assess the fish in front of them. Japan’s koi promotion association gives the most weight to body conformation and also describes skin and colour quality, the fish’s finish on the day and its pattern.

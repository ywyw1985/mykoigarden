Variety Guide

Tancho

Tancho koi typically have a single red patch on the head. On Tancho Kohaku the rest of the fish is white. Tancho Sanke and Tancho Showa can still have black markings, with red confined to the head.

How to identify Tancho koi

In everyday koi usage, Tancho on its own often means Tancho Kohaku. The Japanese Agricultural Standard (JAS) also uses Tancho for the head marking across several varieties. That marking is usually round and red, although the standard allows other colors.

Compare two Tancho in photos

VarietyWhat to look for
Tancho KohakuWhite body with a red head patch.
Tancho SankeA red head patch and Sanke-style black spots (sumi) on the white body.
Tancho ShowaA red head patch with the broader, often wrapping black pattern typical of Showa. Black can also appear on the head.

Tancho Kohaku or Maruten Kohaku?

Look down the back of the fish. A Kohaku with a separate round red head mark and more red patches on the body is Maruten Kohaku. Sumi on Tancho Sanke or Tancho Showa is part of its black pattern, so use the presence of more red when making this comparison.

Other Tancho combinations

Tancho Goshiki and Tancho Kujaku
Look for Goshiki’s net-like pattern or Kujaku’s metallic sheen and dark scale pattern.
Doitsu Tancho Showa and Doitsu Tancho Kujaku
Doitsu describes the reduced scale coverage.
Gin Rin Tancho Kohaku and Gin Rin Tancho Showa
Gin Rin describes the sparkle on the scales.
Tancho Ochiba and Tancho Budo Goromo
The base variety still shows through, and the head mark may differ in color from classic Tancho Kohaku.
Tancho Kin Showa and Tancho Sakura Ogon
Both retain the metallic sheen of the base variety.

What to look for when choosing Tancho

View the fish from above. Check the placement and outline of the head mark, then look at the body line, skin quality and how the fish swims. If you plan to enter a show, check that event’s class list and entry rules.

Variety Guide

Tancho

Tancho are recognized by a single red marking on the head. The appeal is direct, clean, and easy to understand from above.

Award-winning reference photos

Identity

Tancho is a head-pattern category layered on an underlying variety. Common examples include Tancho Kohaku, Tancho Sanke, Tancho Showa, Tancho Goshiki, and Ginrin Tancho Showa.

For everyday recognition, the mark is usually a single round red marking on the head. The complete JAS definition is slightly broader: the circular head imprint can be a color other than scarlet. A koi with a round head mark plus additional body pattern is generally described as Maruten rather than Tancho.

What to look for

  • The red head mark should be visually centered and pleasing.
  • The body should still be strong; the head mark alone does not make a good koi.
  • Clean white skin makes the Tancho impression much stronger.

Beginner note

Do not overpay only for a head spot. Check skin quality, swimming behavior, body line, the underlying variety, and whether the mark has clean edges. See the official JAS Tancho definition.

Matsuba: recognizing the pinecone scale pattern

Matsuba describes a repeated dark mark in individual scales. Look along the back first, then check the ground color and metallic sheen to narrow the name.

What to look for

Follow a row of scales.

Start just behind the head and trace several neighboring scales toward the tail. In Matsuba the dark centers repeat in an orderly arrangement. A few isolated spots on the skin give a different impression.

Separate hue from luster.

The association includes nonmetallic red Aka Matsuba and yellow Ki Matsuba under Muji. In the metallic Hikari-muji group, Gin Matsuba has a silvery white sheen and Kin Matsuba a gold sheen. The name Matsuba alone leaves this distinction open.

Compare similar koi

Matsuba or Kujaku?

Check the scale pattern and the larger patches independently. Kujaku combines dark reticulation, metallic sheen and separate red or orange markings over its ground. The Aka Matsuba in this photo carries one red-orange ground beneath the repeating scales.

Matsuba or Ogon?

A plain Yamabuki Ogon appears evenly metallic yellow. A Kin Matsuba also gleams gold, but the dark scale pattern remains visible across the back. The head alone is a poor place to make this comparison.

Looking at a sale photo

Request a clear view of the shoulders and back, preferably as the fish turns in even light. Note the ground color, the presence of sheen and the repeated scale markings separately. Those three observations make a variety label easier to check.

Does Matsuba always mean metallic?

No. A pinecone-like scale pattern occurs in both metallic and nonmetallic forms. Look for luster separately and keep the color prefix when recording the name.

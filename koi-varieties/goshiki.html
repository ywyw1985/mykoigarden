Koi variety guide

Goshiki Koi

Goshiki is a koi variety with red markings and a netlike scale pattern across the back. Look at the ground color between the red patches: the Asagi-like pattern there is the most useful clue.

Which variety is this koi?

How to identify Goshiki

The Japanese standard JAS 0020 describes a blue to indigo ground with a net pattern formed by the scales across the back, excluding the head. The red patches may show some blue or indigo, and some fish also have black markings. The name means “five colors.” The scale pattern and where it sits are better clues than a color count.

Two Goshiki examples

Goshiki, Koromo and Kujaku compared

VarietyWhere to look
Goshiki KoiThe net pattern runs across the ground of the back, between the red patches.
Koromo (Goromo)Look for blue or black edging on the scales within the red patches. The white ground lacks the Asagi-like net pattern.
KujakuLook for whole-body metallic luster alongside the reticulation and red markings. Check the head and fins as well as the back.

What to look for when choosing a Goshiki

Start with a balanced body and steady swimming. In even light, look for a regular net pattern and clear contrast between the ground and the red. A reflection from a wet scale can look metallic in a photo, so look at the fish from more than one angle.

Names you may see

The All Japan Nishikigoi Promotion Association lists Mameshibori Goshiki and Kuro Goshiki among the names in use. Goshiki-sanshoku and Goshiki-showa combine an Asagi-like ground with the appearance of Taisho Sanke and Showa, respectively.

Koi Varieties

Learn the language of koi color and pattern.

Start with Gosanke, then compare the major non-Gosanke groups: Tancho, Utsurimono, Bekko, Asagi, metallic koi, and other collector categories.

Gosanke / Red and whiteKohaku

The foundational white koi with red hi or beni pattern.

 Gosanke / Three colorTaisho Sanke

White base with red pattern and refined black sumi accents.

 Gosanke / Three colorShowa Sanshoku

Powerful black-based impression with red and white markings.

 Head mark / TanchoTancho

Koi defined by a single red mark on the head, prized for clean simplicity.

 Black base / UtsuriUtsurimono

Black-based koi with white, red, or yellow markings: Shiro, Hi, and Ki Utsuri.

 Stepping stones / BekkoBekko

White, red, or yellow koi with sumi markings that sit like stepping stones.

 Blue net pattern / AsagiAsagi

One of the oldest koi types, known for blue reticulation and red side accents.

 Hikari-muji / MetallicOgon

Single-color metallic koi known for shine, body, and presence.

Shusui, Goromo, Goshiki, Kujaku, Kawarimono, Gin Rin, Doitsu, Chagoi, and more.

Classification references: All Japan Nishikigoi Promotion Association lists named Nishikigoi varieties including Kohaku, Taisho-sanshoku, Showa-sanshoku, Tancho, Hikari-muji and others; Zen Nippon Airinkai organizes show appreciation into 17 variety groups.

Kumonryu: reading a changing black-and-white pattern

Kumonryu are Doitsu koi with sweeping fields of black and white. An individual can look very different in two photographs taken months apart.

What to look for

Look for the scale type first.

A Kumonryu has the mostly bare skin of a Doitsu koi. Check the back and sides for a smooth surface; occasional large scales are compatible with the Doitsu trait.

Trace the boundary of the black.

The black, called sumi, can form broad, cloud-like shapes. Entry 2011 in the 2026 standard describes white areas expanding against a fish that originally has a black ground. The amount of white visible at one age can be misleading.

Compare similar koi

Kumonryu or Shiro Utsuri?

Both can show striking black and white. A wraparound pattern alone settles little. Look for the Doitsu skin and the cloudlike character of the black; ask for the breeder’s identification if a smooth-skinned black-and-white fish remains ambiguous.

What does Beni Kumonryu mean?

It names a related form that also has red markings. Check whether the red stays on the same part of the fish as it turns; a reflection from a neighboring orange fish can be deceptive.

Comparing two photographs

Ask for dated images of the same fish before treating a listing as a promise of its adult pattern. Match landmarks such as the outline of the pectoral fins and compare a similar angle. The Japanese standard records that some fish change their pattern over a short period as their environment changes.

Can the amount of black alone confirm a Kumonryu?

No. Record the scale type and how the black occupies the body. A single frame shows one stage of a variable pattern.

Names to compare in the atlas

  • Kumonryu · Cloudlike black markings spread across smooth white skin. Their outlines can change noticeably over time.
  • Beni Kumonryu · Scarlet patches add a third color to the smooth black-and-white body of a Kumonryu.
  • Matsukawabake · The black patches can shift, as in Kumonryu. Regular scales covering the flanks distinguish Matsukawabake.
  • Kikokuryu · Kumonryu-like black clouds spread across pale, largely scaleless skin with an overall metallic sheen.

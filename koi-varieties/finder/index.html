Find a koi by its markings

Start with one feature you can see clearly. A red, white and black koi might be Sanke or Showa; the shape of the black marks can help you choose which guide to read first.

What can you see?

Leave uncertain features open. The filters cover typical examples and selected forms. A brief flash of light on a few scales is different from a metallic sheen across the head and body.

Guides to compare

18 possible matches

No guide here matches all the selected features. Try removing a feature, especially if glare or distance makes it hard to see.

Kohaku

Look for red islands on an open white ground. If some markings are genuinely black, compare Sanke next.

Taisho Sanke

On a white ground, separate black markings sit among larger red patches. Compare the size and placement of the black with Showa.

Showa Sanshoku

Black can sweep from the flanks toward the back among red and white areas. Some Showa have a great deal of white.

Utsurimono

Look for broad dark shapes alternating with white, yellow or red. Shiro Utsuri is the black-and-white member of this group.

Kumonryu

On nearly bare skin, black and white can meet in cloud-like shapes. A photograph from another season may show a different pattern.

Bekko

Black spots sit apart on a white, red or yellow ground. Compare the spaces between spots with the broader shapes of Utsuri.

Asagi

Trace the blue-gray net across the back, then check the sides and fin bases for warm red. A full net helps separate Asagi from Shusui.

Shusui

Look along the spine for rows of large dark scales. The open flanks can carry red and show much more exposed skin than Asagi.

Ogon

One main color carries a metallic luster across the head and fins. Compare golden Yamabuki Ogon with the silvery Platinum Ogon.

Hariwake

Warm yellow, gold or orange patches interrupt a bright platinum ground. Check that the shine extends beyond the patches.

Matsuba

Dark centers in repeated scales give the back a pinecone texture. The underlying hue and metallic shine vary with the form.

Kujaku

Look for a metallic body, warm patches and dark centers to the scales. Doitsu Kujaku show fewer scales to read.

Goshiki

Red islands lie against a cool, sometimes very dark ground. Compare the network outside the red with the shading inside Goromo patches.

Goromo / Koromo

White skin frames colored patches. Inspect the scales within those patches for blue, purple or dark edging over the red.

Chagoi

A tea-brown or olive body reads as one broad color. Shade can change its appearance, so compare it beside a gray Soragoi.

Soragoi

Look for an even blue-gray impression. Fine scale outlines may show without forming large islands of another color.

Ochiba Shigure

Brown islands float on a gray ground like autumn leaves. Look for two fields of color rather than the even impression of Chagoi.

Hikari Utsuri group

Dark Utsuri- or Showa-like markings combine with a broad metallic shine. Check the head and fins to separate skin luster from water glare.

A second name can describe a trait

Doitsu
Smooth flanks or a few lines of large scales describe the scale form. A Doitsu koi can also be Sanke, Showa or Hariwake.
Gin Rin
Individual scales flash as the fish turns. Use skin sheen in the filters only if the head and skin have a continuous metallic glow.
Tancho
A distinct crown mark can accompany different patterns. Tancho Kohaku and Tancho Showa share the head clue but differ elsewhere.
Longfin / Hirenaga
Elongated fins describe the silhouette. Use the body colors and markings to compare the underlying variety.

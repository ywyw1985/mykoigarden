Cross-cutting scale trait

Doitsu

Doitsu refers to German-scaled koi: fish with few scales, mirror scales, or a largely smooth body. It is a scale type that can modify many varieties.

Award-winning reference photos

How to identify Doitsu

  • Smooth skin with few regular scales.
  • Mirror scales may run along the dorsal line or lateral line.
  • Can appear as Doitsu Kohaku, Doitsu Sanke, Shusui, and many other forms.

Quality points

  • Scale rows should be neat and intentional, not messy.
  • Smooth exposed skin makes scars, uneven color, and body faults easier to see.
  • JNPA highlights clear kiwa—the boundary between exposed ground color and pattern—as an important Doitsu quality point.
  • The fish still needs the correct pattern and quality for its base variety.

Base variety first, scale type second

Doitsu describes scale type rather than a color pattern. Official JAS examples include Doitsu Kohaku, Doitsu Taisho Sanke, Doitsu Showa, Doitsu Shiro Utsuri, Doitsu Goshiki, and Doitsu Ochiba Shigure. Identify the underlying variety first, then add the Doitsu scale modifier.

“Scaleless” is useful shorthand, but not a complete description: most of the body is exposed skin, while larger scales may remain along the back and sides.

Doitsu scale morphology

“Doitsu” does not always mean a perfectly scaleless fish. Historical Japanese association material distinguishes several scale expressions, while the current JAS definition keeps them within the broader Doitsugoi category.

Morphology termWhat it describesClassification caution
Mirror-scaledLarge residual scales, commonly arranged along the back or sides of an otherwise exposed body.Judge the location, row regularity, and consistency of the remaining scales—not scale count alone.
Leather-typeA nearly scaleless expression with extensive exposed skin.Clean skin, color boundaries, and the underlying variety remain important.
Armor-typeAn intermediate expression with heavier or irregular scale coverage.This is morphology terminology, not three separate modern JAS show classes.

Officially documented combinations

JNPA’s current Doitsugoi guide confirms that the scale type appears across many established identities:

  • Gosanke and black-pattern families: Doitsu Kohaku, Doitsu Taisho Sanke, Doitsu Showa Sanshoku, Doitsu Shiro Utsuri, Doitsu Hi Utsuri, Doitsu Ki Utsuri, Doitsu Shiro Bekko, and Doitsu Aka Bekko.
  • Reticulated and patterned families: Doitsu Goshiki, Doitsu Ochiba Shigure, Doitsu Ai-goromo, and Doitsu Budo-goromo.
  • Single-color and Kawarigoi examples: Doitsu Midorigoi, Doitsu Kigoi, Doitsu Chagoi, Doitsu Karashigoi, and Doitsu Karasugoi.

These are verified combinations, not evidence that every imaginable base variety and modifier pairing has an established official name.

When a composition has a recognized name

Do not create a variety name by simply joining “Doitsu,” “metallic,” and a base variety. Several combinations already have established names:

Recognized nameAppearance compositionIdentification cue
KikusuiDoitsu body + Kohaku appearance + whole-body metallic lusterExposed skin and red-and-white Kohaku-style pattern, with luster across the body rather than isolated reflective scales.
Doitsu Yamatonishiki / Heisei NishikiDoitsu body + Taisho Sanke appearance + whole-body metallic lusterWhite, red, and black Sanke-style pattern on exposed Doitsu skin, with metallic shine. Heisei Nishiki is an established alternate name.
Doitsu Hariwake OgonDoitsu body + Hariwake Ogon appearanceExposed Doitsu skin with the metallic gold-and-silver pattern character of Hariwake.
KikokuryuMetallic luster + Kumonryu appearanceMetallic Doitsu-type black-and-white appearance; the current black pattern should not be assumed to remain fixed for life.
Beni KikokuryuKikokuryu appearance + red markingsRed pattern is added to the metallic Kikokuryu identity.
Kin KikokuryuKikokuryu appearance + golden markingsGolden markings distinguish it from the red-marked Beni Kikokuryu.
KinsuiWhole-body metallic luster + Shusui appearanceRetains the recognizable Shusui identity while adding overall metallic shine.

Ginrin is different: Ginrin describes gold or silver reflection from individual scales. The names above depend on whole-body metallic luster and a recognized underlying appearance.

Common comparison

Doitsu is a cross-cutting scale type, while Shusui is a named variety that combines Doitsu scale type with Asagi ancestry and characteristic color placement.

Look first atGood signBeginner caution
BodyBalanced frame, smooth swimming, no deformity.Do not let rare color hide weak conformation.
Skin and colorClean, readable, and consistent for the variety.Muddy color usually becomes more distracting with size.
Scale or luster traitEven rows, sparkle, reticulation, or metallic quality depending on the group.Random patches, dull fins, or broken scale rows reduce impression.

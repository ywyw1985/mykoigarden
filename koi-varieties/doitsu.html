Koi variety guide

Doitsu Koi

A Doitsu koi has very few scales over most of its body, leaving broad areas of skin exposed. Some fish retain rows of large scales along the back or sides. Look at the flanks as well as the back, then identify the underlying color pattern.

Where to look for scales

JAS 0020 describes Doitsugoi by its mostly exposed skin. You may see large, mirror-like scales along the back or sides, or an almost bare body. The remaining scales can vary in size and placement. Doitsu occurs with several color patterns. JNPA documents Doitsu Kohaku, Taisho Sanke, Showa, Shiro Utsuri, Goshiki and others.

Doitsu, Shusui and Gin Rin

Look separately at how much of the body is covered in scales, the color pattern and how it reflects light.

NameWhat to look for
DoitsuSparse scales and exposed skin. In Doitsu Sanke, the base pattern has red and black markings on white.
ShusuiA named variety with Asagi coloration and Doitsu scaling. Look for the blue body and prominent scales along the back.
Gin RinGold or silver flashes from individual scales. The term describes reflection, so look separately at how densely the body is scaled.

For shows, check the rules for that edition. The 62nd ZNA International Koi Show lists Doitsu Kin Showa in Hikari Utsuri.

What to look for when choosing a Doitsu

Start with the body shape and variety pattern. If rows of large scales are present, look at how evenly they run. The JNPA also calls attention to kiwa, the edge where a colored marking meets the ground color.

Doitsu scale morphology

“Doitsu” does not always mean a perfectly scaleless fish. Historical Japanese association material distinguishes several scale expressions, while the current JAS definition keeps them within the broader Doitsugoi category.

Morphology termWhat it describesClassification caution
Mirror-scaledLarge residual scales, commonly arranged along the back or sides of an otherwise exposed body.Judge the location, row regularity, and consistency of the remaining scales—not scale count alone.
Leather-typeA nearly scaleless expression with extensive exposed skin.Clean skin, color boundaries, and the underlying variety remain important.
Armor-typeAn intermediate expression with heavier or irregular scale coverage.Check whether the individual meets the Doitsugoi definition before using that classification.

Officially documented combinations

JNPA’s current Doitsugoi guide confirms that the scale type appears across many established identities:

  • Gosanke and black-pattern families: Doitsu Kohaku, Doitsu Taisho Sanke, Doitsu Showa Sanshoku, Doitsu Shiro Utsuri, Doitsu Hi Utsuri, Doitsu Ki Utsuri, Doitsu Shiro Bekko, and Doitsu Aka Bekko.
  • Reticulated and patterned families: Doitsu Goshiki, Doitsu Ochiba Shigure, Doitsu Ai-goromo, and Doitsu Budo-goromo.
  • Single-color and Kawarigoi examples: Doitsu Midorigoi, Doitsu Kigoi, Doitsu Chagoi, Doitsu Karashigoi, and Doitsu Karasugoi.

These are verified combinations, not evidence that every imaginable base variety and modifier pairing has an established official name.

When a composition has a recognized name

Do not create a variety name by simply joining “Doitsu,” “metallic,” and a base variety. Several combinations already have established names:

Recognized nameAppearance compositionIdentification cue
Sakura Ogon / Platinum KohakuKohaku appearance + whole-body metallic lusterA scaled metallic red-and-white identity; Platinum Kohaku is an alternate name, not a separate phenotype.
KikusuiDoitsu body + Kohaku appearance + whole-body metallic lusterExposed skin and red-and-white Kohaku-style pattern, with luster across the body rather than isolated reflective scales.

Doitsu Yamatonishiki / Heisei NishikiDoitsu body + Taisho Sanke appearance + whole-body metallic lusterWhite, red, and black Sanke-style pattern on exposed Doitsu skin, with metallic shine. Heisei Nishiki is an established alternate name.
Hariwake OgonScaled white body + golden markings + whole-body metallic lusterRegular scales distinguish the base Hariwake identity from its Doitsu counterpart.
Doitsu Hariwake OgonDoitsu body + Hariwake Ogon appearanceExposed Doitsu skin with the metallic white-and-gold pattern character of Hariwake.
KumonryuDoitsu body + black-and-white, cloud-like patternThe sumi may change as the fish grows or its environment changes, so identify the structure rather than one frozen pattern.

Name-tree shortcut: Kumonryu is the Doitsu black-and-white base. Add red and it becomes Beni Kumonryu; add whole-body metallic luster and it becomes Kikokuryu. From Kikokuryu, red markings indicate Beni Kikokuryu and golden markings indicate Kin Kikokuryu. “Kin” describes the golden pattern, not merely stronger shine.

Classification caution: JNPA’s current directory places Kikokuryu in Kawarigoi while also tagging it for Doitsugoi and Hikarimono traits. For competition entries, check the event rules separately from the JNPA directory and the JAS definitions.

Ginrin is different: Ginrin describes gold or silver reflection from individual scales. The names above depend on whole-body metallic luster and a recognized underlying appearance.

Common comparison

Shusui has the appearance of Asagi with Doitsu scaling. The color placement helps identify it.

Look first atGood signBeginner caution
BodyBalanced frame, smooth swimming, no deformity.Do not let rare color hide weak conformation.
Skin and colorClean, readable, and consistent for the variety.Muddy color usually becomes more distracting with size.
Scale or luster traitEven rows, sparkle, reticulation, or metallic quality depending on the group.Random patches, dull fins, or broken scale rows reduce impression.

Names to compare in the atlas

  • Doitsu · Much of the skin is exposed. Check for rows of large scales left along the spine or sides. This scale type occurs in many varieties.
  • Wagoi · Regular scales cover nearly the entire body. Follow a row from the back down the flank to distinguish it from Doitsu scaling.
  • Doitsu Kujaku · Red patches show clearly against bright metallic skin. Check the few remaining scales for the dark accents of Kujaku.
  • Doitsu Showa · Look for bold black running through red and white. Broad, smooth flanks distinguish it from fully scaled Showa.

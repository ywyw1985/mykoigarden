Hariwake: finding the gold pattern on a metallic white koi

Hariwake carries gold-colored patches on a bright white metallic ground. The full effect combines two colors with luster across the body.

What to look for

Find the two colors.

Start at a gold patch and follow its edge into white skin. A warm reflection on the water can look yellow; a real marking stays with the same part of the fish as it swims.

Check the shine across the body.

The bright metallic surface runs through both the pale ground and the colored areas. JNPA places Hariwake in Hikari-moyo, the patterned metallic group, and distinguishes fully scaled Hariwake from the mostly bare-skinned Doitsu form.

Compare similar koi

Hariwake or Yamabuki Ogon?

Both can look golden from across the pond. Yamabuki Ogon is a single metallic yellow; Hariwake has distinct white areas between its golden markings. Watch for a white area that stays visible as the fish turns.

Hariwake or Kikusui?

JNPA describes Kikusui as a metallic Doitsu koi with the red-and-white appearance of Kohaku. Hariwake has gold on a white ground. Warm lighting and orange-toned patches can make the boundary hard to judge from one image.

Reading the name on a listing

Ask for a short view from above as the fish changes angle. Record its two colors, the reach of the metallic shine and its scale coverage before considering fin length. These details tell you much more than a single bright reflection.

Must a Hariwake be Doitsu or long-finned?

No. Those words describe scale coverage and fin shape. The underlying Hariwake identification comes from the metallic white-and-gold pattern.

Names to compare in the atlas

  • Hariwake · Gold patches interrupt the metallic white ground. The scaled form has an even covering of scales along the flanks.
  • Doitsu Hariwake · It shares Hariwake’s white-and-gold pattern, but the flanks show broad stretches of exposed skin.
  • Kikusui · Red patches sit on lustrous white skin. The broad smooth flanks distinguish it from scaled Sakura Ogon.

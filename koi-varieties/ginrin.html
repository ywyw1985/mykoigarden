Koi variety guide

Gin Rin Koi

Gin Rin describes gold or silver flashes from individual koi scales. Start by identifying the fish’s base variety, then watch the scales as the fish turns. The sparkle follows the scales.

How to spot Gin Rin

Look for repeated flashes on individual scales under even light. A metallic (Hikari) koi shows a sheen over the skin, head and fins. Watching the koi from more than one angle helps distinguish both effects from a single bright reflection in a photo. Both traits can occur on the same fish. For example, the JNPA lists Gin Rin Kujaku.

Gin Rin Showa photo

A-Ginrin and B-Ginrin

The Japanese Agricultural Standard JAS 0020 groups Gin Rin by the underlying variety, not by how bright the scales look. Gold and silver reflections can both occur.

JAS groupWhat to look for
A-GinrinThe underlying koi has the appearance of Kohaku, Taisho Sanke or Showa Sanshoku.
B-GinrinGin Rin on other varieties, such as Shiro Utsuri, Goshiki, Asagi or Ochiba Shigure.

Other B-Ginrin examples include Gin Rin Ai-goromo, Budo-goromo, Kumonryu, Chagoi, Soragoi and Karashigoi.

Show entry classes can differ by event. For example, the 62nd ZNA International Koi Show lists Gin Rin Tancho in its Tancho class.

What to look for when choosing a fish

Check body shape and the base pattern, then look at how evenly the sparkling scales are distributed along the back. The JNPA also describes Diamond Gin Rin for strong sparkle and Pearl Gin Rin for a rounded shine near the center of a scale.

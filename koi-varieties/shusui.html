Shusui Koi

Shusui is a koi variety with the blue-and-red appearance of Asagi on a mostly exposed, Doitsu-scaled body. Large scales run along the back. Look at their arrangement, the blue ground and the position of the red markings together.

Look at the back and the flanks

JAS 0020 defines Shusui as Asagi with the appearance of Doitsugoi. The JNPA highlights its blue body, large dorsal scales, a clear head and balanced red on the belly, fins and cheeks. The dorsal scales are called onigoke in Japanese.

Shusui, Asagi and Doitsu compared

First look at how the scales cover the body, then at the ground color and markings.

NameWhat to look for
ShusuiMostly exposed skin with the Asagi appearance. Large scales line the back, and red markings may appear on the flanks and belly.
AsagiScales cover the body and form a net pattern on the blue back. Red commonly appears on the abdomen.
DoitsuA term for very sparse scaling and exposed skin across several color patterns. Use the ground color and markings to identify Shusui.

What to look for when choosing Shusui

Start with a balanced body and steady swimming. Look for evenly aligned dorsal scales of consistent size, a clear head and readable contrast between the blue ground and red markings. Judge the whole fish from more than one angle.

Names you may encounter

Hana Shusui
Red markings lie between the dorsal and lateral scale lines. The JNPA also describes a series of rounded red markings.
Hi Shusui
The red extends farther up onto the back.
Ki Shusui
The ZNA describes a yellow form that retains blue or indigo ground. Its guide distinguishes yellow Doitsu fish with black dorsal scales and no indigo ground as Doitsu Ki Matsuba.
Pearl Shusui
The large dorsal scales show Gin Rin reflections.
Ginsui and Kinsui
The ZNA Shusui reference places these metallic relatives in Hikari-moyomono.

The ZNA reference places Shusui Sanshoku, Shusui Showa and Goshiki Shusui in Kawarimono. Check the entry rules for the specific show and edition before entering a fish.

Names to compare in the atlas

  • Shusui · A line of large blue scales follows the spine. The smooth flanks often carry red below that line.
  • Hana Shusui · Rounded red patches repeat along a Shusui, above its smooth flanks and beside the dorsal scales.
  • Hi Shusui · Red climbs the smooth sides all the way to the dorsal line of scales.
  • Asagi · Look for a blue net of scales across the back. Red often shows low on the flanks, at the cheeks and in the fins.

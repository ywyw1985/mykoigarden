Variety guide

Showa Sanshoku

Showa Sanshoku is a black-based koi with red and white markings. Continuous, wrapping sumi helps distinguish it from Sanke; head markings and pectoral-fin black are supporting clues, not a single-feature test.

Award-winning reference photos

How to recognize Showa

The JNPA definition describes a black ground with red and white markings and continuous utsuri-zumi. Visible black need not occupy most of the fish. Keep variety identification separate from preferences for a striking or balanced show pattern.

FeatureWhat to look forHow to use it
SumiConnected black areas that may wrap up from below the lateral line.Look at how the pattern continues around the body, not only the size of each spot.
Head markingsHead sumi and menware can contribute to the characteristic pattern.Consider them with the whole fish; a head mark alone is not decisive.
Pectoral finsMotoguro is concentrated black at the fin base.Its visibility can change with development; it is not a mandatory standalone test.
DevelopmentSumi and the overall pattern may change as young fish mature.Ask for age and earlier photographs; future appearance is not guaranteed.

Showa and Sanke: compare several clues

ClueShowaSanke
Base colourBlack base with red and white markings.White base with red and black markings.
Sumi patternContinuous utsuri-zumi, often wrapping upward.Black commonly appears as more separate accents.
Head and finsHead sumi and motoguro are useful clues.Often a cleaner head and pectoral stripes; exceptions occur.

Do not classify from head sumi, motoguro or a fixed colour percentage alone. Use the whole pattern and reliable history where the photograph is inconclusive.

A real Showa reference

Terms worth knowing

Motoguro (元黒)
A concentrated black area at the base of a pectoral fin.
Menware (面割れ)
Head sumi that visually divides the face. Exact pattern labels vary between references; do not require one rigid shape.
Utsuri-zumi (写り墨)
Continuous black-base areas involved in the overall wrapping pattern.

Where Kindai Showa fits

Kindai Showa describes a modern Showa appearance with more visible white and less visible black. It remains within Showa, rather than a separate base variety in the cited definition. A white-dominant photograph does not by itself make a fish Sanke; judge the underlying pattern rather than inventing a white-percentage cutoff.

Choosing a young Showa

  • Begin with body shape, skin and overall balance rather than one dramatic mark.
  • Compare images taken at different ages; sumi development is not a promise of a particular result.
  • Use clear views of the head, both pectoral fins and body together.

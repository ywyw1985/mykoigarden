How to read a koi show result

At the 2025 British National Koi Show, an 82 cm Kohaku earned Grand Champion. An 83 cm Kohaku at the very same show received a different award. How can both results be right?

Three fish from one show

Reported lengths in centimetres. Each bar uses the same scale.

  • Grand Champion · Kohaku 82 cm
  • Mature Champion · Kohaku 83 cm
  • Jumbo Champion · Mukashi Ogon 103 cm

Official UK results

A name, a length and a title

Find the event and year first. The award tells you which contest the fish won. The variety describes the fish or its judging class. A length is a measurement only when the unit is stated. In these British results, Grand Champion and Jumbo Champion went to different fish.

UK judging guide

What does a size class mean?

In the 2026 IWGKS results, Katy Shanafelt’s Kohaku is recorded in size class 6, with fish ID 90 and tank 18. The numbers name a class, an individual entry and a tank respectively. To find a length range you need the rules for that event.

What does a Japanese show card add?

At the 55th All Japan show in Tokyo in January 2025, the winning Showa was listed in the 95-bu class. The record names Yuan Jiandong as owner, Dainichi as breeder and handler, and Sanshin Trading as dealer. Those roles describe different parts of the fish’s history.

Which of the three British winners was the longest?

The 103 cm Mukashi Ogon. Read its award alongside its length: it won Jumbo Champion. The overall Grand Champion was the 82 cm Kohaku.

Does the American entry tell us that the Kohaku was 90 cm long?

90 is its fish ID. The length field in this record gives size class 6. Look up the show’s measuring rules before assigning centimetres.

Chinese koi farms, their show winners and bloodlines

When Xiao Jinhong won in Jiangmen, the show report named both her breeder in Guangdong and her champion mother. In Ningbo, another Grand Champion can be traced to a named line, but her parents’ names were never given in the show report. These four farm records show how far the evidence takes us.

Changlong · Guo Bin · Xianhu · Suxin

Changlong Koi Farm · Shunde, Guangdong

On 14 January 2024, Xiao Jinhong (小锦鸿), bred at Changlong, won Grand Champion at the seventh China Domestic Koi Show in Jiangmen. The show report identifies her as Jinhong’s daughter. A 94 cm fish from the same farm took second place.

Mother
Jinhong (锦鸿) was a Kohaku of Dainichi’s Xjr line. Owned by Pan Zhicheng, she won the overall title at the 51st ZNA International Koi Show and later served as a broodfish at Changlong.
Daughter
Xiao Jinhong was bred in Shunde and won her own overall title in China. The report names her mother and leaves her sire unidentified.

Jiangmen show report and photographs of mother and daughter · Report on Jinhong at Changlong

Changlong was established in Ronggui in 2001. When Guangdong recognized it as the province’s first certified koi breeding-stock farm in March 2025, Guangzhou Daily reported 6,500 fish across ten lines.

Guangzhou Daily farm profile, March 2025

Guo Bin Koi Farm · Ningbo, Zhejiang

A 103 cm Kohaku bred and raised at Guo Bin Koi Farm in Yinjiang won overall Grand Champion at the 21st China Koi Show in December 2021. Ningbo’s local news report identifies it as the first domestically bred overall winner in that show’s history.

Breeder and owner
Guo Bin’s farm bred the fish. The show report names Wang Wei (王威) as its owner.
Bloodline
The event report identifies the winner as Taiyuan (太原系). It gives neither parent’s individual name.

Ningbo report from the farm, 2021 · Contemporary show report on the line and owner

A 2025 association visit recorded more than 600 broodfish across more than ten lines. Its inventory included Yulan (玉兰) and Julang (巨浪), examples of the wider breeding collection.

Chinese recreational angling association farm visit, 2025

Xianhu Koi Farm · Zhongshan, Guangdong

On 1 June 2006, a two-year-old Kohaku hatched and raised at Xianhu won the 60 size division at the second China Young Koi Show in Shunde. The official account gives the division, rather than an individual measurement.

Breeder and exhibitor
Farm owner Chen Jinsheng (陈锦胜) entered the young koi. He told the reporter that this group had hatched locally.
Known parent
The winning Kohaku’s mother was a five-year-old female from broodstock Chen imported from Japan. The account gives no individual name for her or the sire.

In 2006 Chen’s operations in Minzhong and Banfu together covered 1,000 mu, about 67 hectares. That figure describes the two locations at the time of the interview.

Fisheries Bureau archive of the June 2006 report

Suxin Koi Farm · Suzhou, Jiangsu

Suxin’s breeding diary follows a Sakai-bred Kohaku that won the 2018 China Koi Show. The farm recorded her as ten years old and 98 cm at the time of the June 2019 spawning in Suzhou. Her own mother was Rose Symbol. The breeding report’s photograph matches the Kohaku identified as Sakura Queen in Samurai Koi Centre’s award archive.

On 9 June 2019, the farm used two brothers as sires. Both were sons of S Legend. For a juvenile from that batch, the record identifies its mother and both possible fathers. It leaves open which brother sired that juvenile.

Suxin breeding diary, reproduced by Mountain Tree

In 2019, Farmers’ Daily reported that Suxin operated three sites covering 150 mu, about ten hectares, for broodstock, growing young fish and sales.

Farmers’ Daily profile, July 2019 (PDF)

The next generation at Jiangmen

In January 2026, more than 100 farms entered over 2,600 domestically bred koi in the ninth China Domestic Koi Show. A 91 cm male Showa took overall Grand Champion. The report names Li Haotong (李濠僮) of Zhongshan Chiban as the exhibitor. Its breeder and parents are unlisted.
